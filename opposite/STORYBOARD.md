## 1. The tape in one paragraph

A second-generation dub of *OPPOSITE DAY! A Learn-Along Game Tape* (© 1994 Two-Way Kids Home Video), parts of it recorded over with a stage manager's night camcorder footage. Two felt mirror-twins, BIM and his copy MIB, host from either side of a big empty gold frame in a playroom built perfectly symmetric, and they teach the children's game every viewer already knows: on Opposite Day yes means no, forward is backward, goodbye means hello. BIM points to the little chair on his side: 'This is your spot.' He laughs that MIB's cards are mirror-written, so 'if you can read them, you're on the wrong side.' The tape keeps its rules perfectly, and that is the horror: once the viewer has learned to decode, every reassurance decodes into a threat inside their own head. 'Is everybody here?' 'NO!' - and one more voice answers, backwards. A figure in MIB's doorway is the only thing in the tape that walks forward. A jolly upside-down portrait is turned over and the tape tears away before we see it. The night camcorder's negative effect shows MIB sitting in your chair. When the show comes back, the twins have traded sides and become each other's mirror image, and nobody mentions it. BIM frowns, and MIB's smile is that frown upside down. MIB says 'You're safe' while the lights go out, and the portrait is right way up: its eyes and mouth were upside down all along. MIB teaches the way to end the game - say 'It's NOT Opposite Day!' - and the children chant it, and 'on Opposite Day, that means...'. Behind the set there is another, darker Turnaround Room, and in the gold frame's mirror the one holding the camera is MIB. MIB says goodbye (hello): 'BIM isn't with you. He isn't right behind you.' The dead TV on MIB's side reflects a living room, and on the couch someone small kneels with its back to the screen; it is BIM, and his face was upside down too. The VCR rewinds by itself, and the whisper we heard backwards plays forwards: 'Your turn to be the reflection.' PLAY comes back mirrored. From MIB's side of the frame we look at the empty Chair For You; the blanket is on the chair we are sitting in, and MIB's card is readable. 'Bye!' '...I mean hello.' Two knocks on the glass. TODAY IS NOT OPPOSITE DAY.

### Escalation curve

- **0:00-1:57.1 Act 1, trust (with seeds).** A bright, sweet, symmetric kids' tape. Every wrongness is small enough to doubt: reversed bars, a mirrored logo, an anticlockwise clock, a lyric that does not match its caption, a silent copy 4 frames late, one extra 'NO!', a figure walking forward in a doorway, a duck on the wrong chest. The only dread spike is the withheld portrait turn (6d-6e).
- **1:57.1-2:53.7 Act 2, slippage.** Recorded-over night footage (the held negative, the memo), then the show resumes with the swap: the rules start applying to the viewer ('safe means not safe'). The 'You're safe' rhythm drains the light into scare 1.
- **2:53.7-4:40.0 Act 3, descent.** Bright and cheerful again, which is worse: MIB alone, BIM gone, the blanket moved, the paradox lock. ## 4. Characters (design bible for the canvas artists)

All characters, the label and the show are ORIGINAL (nothing resembling any existing kids' show, puppet franchise, analog-horror series, real TV station or agency). PG-13: no blood, gore, wounds or harm to anyone; the horror is wrongness, inversion and realism in the wrong place.

**BIM** (`bim_*`, Set A, the LEFT side until 8a). BIM: a full-body felt puppet boy (Muppet-style, about 1.1 m tall), Easter-yellow felt skin (#F4CF4A, visible fibre texture: noise fill plus hundreds of 1-3 px fibre strokes, blur(0.6px)), head 12% too big for the body, a brown felt BOWL HAIRCUT (#6B4226) with its parting on HIS left (screen right), round glossy black BUTTON EYES with a catchlight at the same pixel in both, a small round orange felt nose, rosy cheek circles, a SEWN-ON SMILE (a running-stitch curve in dark red thread #6E1020, corners turned up, each corner finished with a small upward 'tuck' stitch like a dimple), a red-and-white horizontally striped shirt (#D9473B / #FFF4E2), a pink felt HEART PATCH on HIS left chest (screen right), royal-blue felt shorts, white socks, red sneakers, four-fingered yellow hands with fingers 1.3x too long, and a felt POINTER (a yellow dowel with a red felt star tip) in his RIGHT hand (screen left).

**MIB** (`mib_*`, Set B, the RIGHT side until 8a). MIB: BIM's exact mirror twin. Draw him by running BIM's drawing function mirrored left/right (the parting on HIS right = screen left, the heart patch on HIS right chest = screen left, the pointer in his LEFT hand = screen right), with ONE deliberate difference: his MOUTH is BIM's FROWN (see bim_close_frown) flipped upside down. So it curves up like a smile, but everything that belongs below a frown is above it: the little puckered-chin stitch (a small '^' of thread that sits under the centre of BIM's frown) sits ABOVE the centre of MIB's mouth, between mouth and nose, and the corner tuck stitches point down-and-in instead of up-and-out. At a glance it is a smile; once you have seen BIM frown it is a frown turned over. His button eyes have the catchlight at the same pixel as BIM's (mirrored). He never speaks with his own voice. In Act 1 his mouth flaps silently 4 frames after BIM's. After the swap BIM's bright voice comes out of HIS mouth, and BIM speaks with MIB's lower voice.

**MR. TURNABOUT** (`turnabout_upside`, `turnabout_scare`). A little gouache portrait of a rosy, bald, white-whiskered grandfather in a blue bow tie, hung upside down on Set A's wall. His eyes and mouth were painted the right way up for the viewer, so upside down he reads as jolly (the Thatcher illusion). Turned right way up, the eyes and mouth are inverted: wet, real, pinpoint eyes with the lids the wrong way round and a down-turned grimace of real ivory teeth with the gums underneath. No wounds or blood: the wrongness is only the inversion and the realism.

**THE CHAIR FOR YOU.** A small white child's chair with a red heart cut out of its backrest and a knitted cream blanket with a red zig-zag band. 'This is your spot, right here on my side!' (4b). From 9a a second identical chair stands on MIB's side and the blanket is on it; in 12c we are sitting in it.

**THE FIGURE** (`hidden_twin_stand`). A near-black child-height silhouette with the twins' bowl-cut head, arms to the knees, head tilted 15 degrees, two pinpoint glints for eyes. Never lit, never acknowledged.

**THE TURNAROUND KIDS.** The unseen studio audience of children (laugh track, cheers, chants). Late in the tape the laugh track runs backwards and becomes long inhales.

**TWO-WAY KIDS HOME VIDEO.** Fictional 1994 label. Logo: a red left-pointing arrow and a blue right-pointing arrow chasing each other round a circle, bubble wordmark. No real-company resemblance; no warning screens of any real kind.

**Drawing rules (from `poppy/research/brief.md` and `scarier.md`).** Keep the felt cute and make ONE thing per frame too real, too still, too dark or in the wrong place. Felt: noise fill plus hundreds of 1-3 px fibre strokes, blur(0.6px), pilling, running stitches 4-6 px with a thread shadow. Realism patches use `poppy/draw/horror.js` (`wetEye`/`eyePP`, `teethRow`/`teethPP`, `gumsPP`, `reliefPP`, `hatch`, `tremor`, `levels`, `budget`). At most 2 proportion breaks per beat before the scares; stack 4-5 in the scare frames. Scare frames: shadow hatching of 200-600 one-pixel strokes at 2-3 angles; tremor contours (0.6-1.5 px jitter, overdrawn 2-4 times); each has a `_b` boil frame with re-seeded hatching whose mean luminance matches within 2%. The Thatcher faces must be drawn so that the UPSIDE-DOWN view reads as a pleasant, normal face (check by rotating the PNG 180 degrees) and the upright view is grotesque. Never blood, gore, wounds, self-harm or harm to anyone.

### Hand-off checklist

- **Rooms** (`opposite/render_rooms.py`, adapted from `poppy/render_rooms.py`; bpy 5.2, Cycles CPU, 32-64 samples, denoise): 12 renders from essentially three scenes - the Turnaround Room (wide, wide_late, frame, frame_dim, portrait, night, crt_set_b, reverse; plus a mirrored duplicate for mirror_studio and a mirror pane for frame_mirror_night), the back of the flats (behind_set) and the living room. Build Set A once and duplicate it with scale x = -1 so the symmetry is exact. Write `build/rooms/rooms_meta.json`. Never put a figure in a room render.
- **Character art** (`opposite/draw/`, adapted from `poppy/draw/`): 25 files; MIB is BIM's drawing function mirrored with the flipped-frown mouth; keep registration identical within each family. Contact-sheet them: the Act 1 frames must look friendly at a glance, the Thatcher faces must look pleasant when rotated 180 degrees, and the scare frames must be the sharpest, most finished images.
- **Misc art:** 17 files; text spelled exactly as given (the 'text' column).
- **Audio** (`opposite/make_audio.py`, adapted from `poppy/make_audio.py`): 78 files, 44.1 kHz mono 16-bit, plus `build/audio/manifest.json` (durations, 'lyrics' for mus_theme, 'phrases' for vo_kids_chant) and `.env.json` mouth envelopes for the voices. Derived stems (sfx_preverb_safe, sfx_laugh_rev, vo_whisper_fwd/rev from one take) must be re-rendered when their source changes.
- **Compositor:** sections 2, 3, 5 and 7. Runtime must stay within 280-320 s; if a voice line comes back longer than its slot, extend that shot's hold and trim a following static shot rather than moving the scare beats.
- **Every generator is incremental** (skip valid existing outputs unless forced or named), saves progress often, and no single process runs longer than ~10 minutes.
